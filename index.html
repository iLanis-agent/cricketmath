🏏 CricketMath

A scorecard full of numbers and none of them say what you want to know: is 7.4 an over rate or a problem? Is 23 off 40 balls an anchor or a hole? And what does "48.3 overs" even mean? CricketMath computes the averages, rates, and the net run rate that tournaments actually get decided by.

  • Batting honestly - average is runs per dismissal, so not-outs do not count against you. Strike rate is runs per hundred balls. Both, from raw numbers.
  • Bowling figures - average, economy, and strike rate, the trio that separates a spell from a spellcheck.
  • The chase - current rate, required rate, and the projected score at this pace. 116 needed off 180 balls is 3.87 an over - a jog, until wickets fall.
  • Net run rate - including the rule everyone forgets: get bowled out early and you are charged your full quota of overs, not the ones you survived.
  • The overs quirk - 48.3 overs is 48 overs and 3 balls, because an over has six balls. The engine converts honestly.
